# Design critique panel: research (2026-10-18)

> **Unverified.** Each deep-dive was meant to be re-checked by a separate fact-checker. All 11 checks failed because the Pro session limit was hit. Until they run, treat every quote, award and date below as unconfirmed.

Method: two agents picked the designers from documented top honours (AIGA Medal, ADC Hall of Fame, TDC Medal, Cooper Hewitt National Design Award and similar). One agent per designer then extracted their published principles and turned them into a critique lens. The lens applies their documented principles; the critic never speaks as them.

## World

### Milton Glaser (1929-2020): Concept and Feeling: one true idea the viewer completes (derived from Milton Glaser's published principles)
*Discipline:* Graphic design, posters, illustration, identity (I Love NY, New York magazine, Push Pin Studios) · *researcher confidence:* 0.85
**Honours:** National Medal of Arts (2009 medal, presented by President Obama at the White House on February 25, 2010). Confirmed on the NEA's official page, which says he "turned ideas, data, and the abstract into immediately recognizable icons like the I (Heart) NY logo". The 'first graphic designer to receive it' claim comes from Wikipedia (fetched: "Glaser is the first graphic designer to have received this award"). The NEA page does not say this, so treat that part as moderately confident. (2009) [https://www.arts.gov/honors/medals/milton-glaser]; AIGA Medal. AIGA's page returned 403 to every fetch, so this rests on a search snippet of aiga.org, which describes the medal as recognising graphic design of beauty, vitality, invention, and humanity. The 1979 ADC Hall of Fame biography (fetched) independently lists 'the American Institute of Graphic Arts Medalist Award' among his awards, with no year given. (1972) [https://www.aiga.org/membership-community/aiga-awards/1972-aiga-medalist-milton-glaser]; Art Directors Club Hall of Fame. The page was fetched and reads '1979 ADC Hall of Fame Inductee'. (1979) [https://creativehalloffame.org/inductees/milton-glaser/]; National Design Award for Lifetime Achievement, Cooper-Hewitt National Design Museum. Confirmed in the fetched Wikipedia page ('In 2004, Glaser won a National Design Award Lifetime Achievement from the Cooper-Hewitt National Design Museum') and in the fetched AP obituary on PBS ('The Cooper-Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum awarded him a lifetime achievement award in 2004'). (2004) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Glaser]
**Core question:** What should the viewer feel, and what should they believe or do afterwards? Does one honest, appropriate idea, decoded in a moment, make them feel it, using just enough elements and no style for its own sake?
**Principles (with evidence):**
- Design is purposeful. Decide what you want to achieve, and in particular what the viewer should feel, then ask how to make them feel it. With I Love NY the goal was a feeling (welcome), not a layout.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "I will certainly try incorporate the intent of the client after all design is purposeful and what must establish what one wants to accomplish. In the case of I heart NY for instance you want people to feel welcome in the city, so I ask ‘OK – how do I make them feel welcome’, etc." [Interview: Milton Glaser (AIGA Dallas, MiHyun Kim, 2015)](https://dallas.aiga.org/interview-milton-glaser/)
- Give the viewer a small puzzle they can solve quickly (his 'ambiguity principle'). The gap between seeing and understanding is critical. If it is too short the viewer isn't engaged; if it is too long they get confused and resentful.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "In our practice we frequently use a less elevated version of the ambiguity principle to create a puzzle that the audience can solve within a short length of time. Clearly, the period of time between seeing something and understanding it is critical, too short and the viewer is not engaged, too long and you lose his attention and frequently generate confusion and resentment. [Same essay:] The brain frequently remains inert until a problem is presented to it." [Ambiguity & Truth (delivered at the AIGA Brand Identity Conference, NYC, June 21, 2004), on miltonglaser.com Essays](https://www.miltonglaser.com/milton/c:essays/)
- 'Just enough is more.' Glaser rejects 'less is more' as dogma. The test is not minimalism but whether every element is essential to the effect.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "If you look at a Persian rug, you cannot say that less is more because you realize that every part of that rug, every change of colour, every shift in form is absolutely essential for its aesthetic success. You cannot prove to me that a solid blue rug is in any way superior. [...] However, I have an alternative to the proposition that I believe is more appropriate. 'Just enough is more.'" [Ten Things I Have Learned, No. 5 'Less is not necessarily more' (part of an AIGA talk in London, November 11, 2001)](https://www.miltonglaser.com/milton/c:essays/)
- Appropriateness over style. Style deserves no loyalty: some jobs need no visible style and some need a strong one. The question is what fits this message and this audience. Ten Things No. 6, 'Style is not to be trusted', says the same ('It's absurd to be loyal to a style. It does not deserve your loyalty.').. Evidence (fetched, quote): "my criteria for most things is appropriateness. [...] there are jobs that require the absence of style, as well as those that require the presence of style. [...] I have the idea that there isn’t any truth in style. it’s very temporal, bound to the moment that we live in and the way we see things." [Milton Glaser designboom interview](https://www.designboom.com/interviews/milton-glaser-designboom-interview/)
- Feeling comes from naming real emotion honestly, including pain. Work moves people when it says what they already feel but can't put into words. In 'I Love NY More Than Ever' he deliberately drew a wounded heart.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "showing a wounded heart is important. to attain healing, the first thing one must do is to acknowledge that there’s pain." [Milton Glaser designboom interview (on I Love NY More Than Ever, 2001)](https://www.designboom.com/interviews/milton-glaser-designboom-interview/)
- Tell the truth. A designer owes the public at least what a butcher owes his customers. Glaser cites a health ad (a testimonial plus a tiny 'results may not be typical' line) as the kind of everyday distortion designers take part in.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "As a designer, do we have less responsibility to our public than a butcher? [...] 'Do no harm' is an admonition to doctors concerning their relationship to their patients, not to their fellow practitioners or the drug companies. [Ambiguity & Truth, same page:] At the bottom of the screen in 6 point, barely visible type, were the words "results may not be typical". [...] Yet we must begin with the presumption that telling the truth is essential for human survival." [Ten Things I Have Learned, No. 10 'Tell the truth' (2001), and Ambiguity & Truth (2004), on miltonglaser.com Essays](https://www.miltonglaser.com/milton/c:essays/)
- Professionalism is not enough: the good is the enemy of the great. Repeating a safe formula is the failure mode, and the field needs risk-taking. The AP obituary on PBS quotes him: 'There's an enormous pressure to repeat past successes. That's a sure death.' It also quotes him judging work as great when it moves you, good when it accomplishes a task, and bad when it misses a target.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "PROFESSIONALISM IS NOT ENOUGH or THE GOOD IS THE ENEMY OF THE GREAT. [...] what is required in our field, more than anything else, is the continuous transgression. Professionalism does not allow for that because transgression has to encompass the possibility of failure and if you are professional your instinct is not to fail, it is to repeat success." [Ten Things I Have Learned, No. 4 (2001)](https://www.miltonglaser.com/milton/c:essays/)
- Words are images. A few well-chosen words can do the work of a picture, which matters directly for kinetic typography. The same talk gives his definition of design as moving from an existing position to a preferred one.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "I have become increasingly interested in words, because I think words are images. I don’t see a separation between words and images. I think the right three words can create as much imagery as any motion picture. [...] Design is simply planning. My definition of design is this: it is going from an existing position to a preferred one. It’s everything." [Inside the Mind of Milton Glaser (Y Magazine, BYU; quotes from his BYU address of Sept. 28, 2006)](https://magazine.byu.edu/article/inside-the-mind-of-milton-glaser/)
**Checks:** Name the feeling and the shift. Before scoring, the critic writes one line: 'from [existing condition] to [preferred condition], feeling [X]'. Example: from 'worried about Mum's blood test and the traffic' to 'relieved, a nurse can come home', feeling reassurance. If the brief or the piece gives no clear answer, the piece cannot score above 6. · One idea. State the idea in 7 Arabic words or fewer. Is there a single visual idea (a metaphor, juxtaposition, symbol or twist) rather than a headline sitting beside loosely related pictures or icons? · Puzzle timing. Is there a small 'aha' the viewer completes themselves? On a static frame or a video's first frame it should land in about 1 to 2 seconds (TabeebX's adaptation, not Glaser's number). Fail it if the picture only repeats the headline literally (nothing to engage) or if it needs the caption to make sense (confusion). · Just enough. Count the elements: headline, sub, image, icons, logo, CTA, decoration. Could any be removed without losing the feeling? Is anything missing that the idea needs, such as a human cue or a concrete Baghdad detail? · Pink as the emotional pivot. Like the single red heart in I Love NY against cool type, the one #EE396B accent should sit on the semantic or emotional centre of the idea (the turning word, the symbol, or the CTA) in one place, against navy #181943, white and blue #1B9CCE. Fail it if pink is used as decoration or appears in several competing places. · Appropriateness over style. Was the look chosen for this message and audience (Iraqi families, elders, worried caregivers) or borrowed from a trend (glossy AI renders, 3D blobs, glassmorphism, generic 'medical blue' templates)? Would the idea survive a change of style? If it wouldn't, the style is doing the work the concept should. · Honest feeling. Does it acknowledge the real situation with dignity (worry, waiting rooms, distance, an ageing parent) rather than fake cheer or fear? Does every image match what TabeebX actually delivers? Check for: no implied outcomes, no AI or stock faces presented as real staff or patients, no fine-print disclaimers. Cross-check with health-ad-policy. · Words as images. Do the 2 to 4 key Arabic words (Cairo Bold) carry imagery by themselves, with Cairo Medium in support? In kinetic typography, does the motion of the words express their meaning, with one key word per beat, rather than animating everything at once? · Beyond professional. Is there one unexpected, ownable move that a competitor's template would not contain? Or is this a competent repeat of the last post?
**Red flags:** Clip-art concept: stethoscope-heart, floating medical icons, smiling stock doctor or generic glossy AI imagery. Any clinic in any country could post it. · Two or more ideas competing in one frame or one Reel. The idea can't be said in one short sentence. · The visual just illustrates the headline word for word (zero puzzle). Or the opposite: viewers need the caption to understand it. · Pink accent in three or more places, or used on decoration instead of the idea. · Faked emotion: grinning models, staged joy. Or manipulative fear: sirens, blood, panic imagery. · Untruthful signals: implied cures or results, invented testimonials, AI people passed off as real staff or patients, tiny 'results may vary' type. · Trend-led style (gradients, particles, 3D blobs) doing the work a concept should. · A Latin layout mechanically mirrored for RTL, so the eye meets the punchline before the setup. · Motion with no meaning: everything animates, nothing is revealed, no setup and payoff.
**Anchors:** 10: A single, ownable idea that makes the named feeling land in the first second or two. The viewer completes the 'aha' and feels it was their own thought. Every element is essential; pink sits exactly on the emotional pivot; RTL reading order delivers setup then payoff. It is truthful and specific to Baghdad families. People would remember it and want to share or save it, and it could not be mistaken for a competitor's post or a template. | 8: A clear, appropriate idea with the right feeling that decodes quickly and is honest. One or two surplus elements, or execution that is slightly generic (the idea is stronger than the craft, or the reverse). A confident 'yes' with a hint of 'wow'. | 6: Competent, on-brand and understood, but literal: headline plus a relevant picture. The viewer is informed rather than moved, and the style is borrowed rather than chosen. 'Good' in the sense that it accomplishes a task. Professional, and forgettable. | 4: The message is only clear with the caption, or the frame is cluttered with competing ideas and decoration. Emotion is generic (stock smiles), pink is scattered, and the style was chosen before the concept. In video, nothing is revealed before the swipe point. | 2: No identifiable idea or feeling, or a feeling achieved by manipulation (fear, fake people, exaggerated or implied claims). Confusing enough to cause resentment, or likely to damage trust in a health brand. It misses the target.
**For TabeebX:** How to apply it: the critic applies these documented principles and must never speak as Glaser. Write 'Under the concept-and-feeling lens (from Glaser's published principles)...', never 'Glaser would say...', and quote only the evidence lines above.

Arabic RTL: the puzzle has to resolve in Arabic reading order. Setup at the top right, payoff towards the lower left. In a carousel, the twist sits on the swipe to the next slide (RTL). Don't import English puns or rebuses. The 'aha' must work in Iraqi dialect for the iraqi-dialect skill. Glaser's I Love NY works because each part must be translated, and an Arabic equivalent has to be decodable by Iraqi viewers, not by English speakers.

Cairo font: treat the 2 to 4 key words as the image ('the right three words', BYU 2006). Cairo Bold for them, Cairo Medium for support. Letterform play (for example, a letter's dot becoming a location pin or a heart) is allowed only if the word stays fully legible and the Arabic letter shapes are respected. No fake kashida stretching or distorted joins.

One pink accent: the direct analogy is the single red heart against neutral American Typewriter letters in I Love NY. The emotional symbol carries the one warm colour. The raised-arms logo figure (relief, welcome) can serve as that symbol, but only when the idea calls for it.

Vertical video: the decoding window is the first 1 to 2 seconds before the swipe, so the setup must be visible immediately and the payoff should come on a clear beat. Motion should reveal the idea (setup then twist), one key word per beat, inside platform safe zones. A Reel that is all motion and no reveal scores 4 or below.

Health truth: 'Tell the truth' and the 'results may not be typical' example map directly onto health-ad-policy. Anything implying results, or using AI or stock people as staff or patients, caps the score at 2 to 4 under this lens, however beautiful it is.

The score anchors (great, good, misses the target) echo Glaser's own scale as quoted by AP in 2000 (PBS obituary): great when it 'moves it in a certain way', good 'when it accomplishes a task', bad 'when it misses a target' (https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/milton-glaser-designer-of-i-love-ny-logo-dies-at-91, fetched).

Lower-confidence sources, used only as colour and not as evidence:
(a) 'There are three responses to a piece of design—yes, no, and WOW! Wow is the one to aim for.' This is attributed to Glaser in Poster House's description of the book 'Yes, No, WOW!' (https://shop.posterhouse.org/yes-no-wow-the-push-pin-studios-revolution.html, fetched). I found no primary source that dates it.

### Paula Scher: Poster Lens: type as image, scale and one accent (drawn from Paula Scher's published principles)
*Discipline:* Graphic design: brand identity, typographic posters, environmental graphics. Partner at Pentagram New York since 1991 (Pentagram's bio, Creative Boom, SEGD and designboom all say 1991, not the 1990 given in the brief). Did the Public Theater identity from 1994. Wrote Make It Bigger (2002), MAPS (2011) and Twenty-Five Years at the Public: A Love Story (2020). · *researcher confidence:* 0.86
**Honours:** AIGA Medal (confirmed: Creative Hall of Fame page and Pentagram bio both say 2001) (2001) [https://creativehalloffame.org/?p=1792]; Art Directors Club Hall of Fame (confirmed: 'In 1998 Scher was named to the Art Directors Club Hall of Fame') (1998) [https://creativehalloffame.org/?p=1792]; Type Directors Club Medal. Confirmed: the Pentagram bio says 'in 2006 she was awarded the Type Directors Club Medal, the first woman to receive the prize'. The Creative Hall of Fame page is also titled '2006 TDC Medalist'. (2006) [https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher]; Cooper-Hewitt National Design Award for Communication Design. Confirmed in Pentagram news dated 9 May 2013, which says she 'was previously a Finalist in the Communication Design category in 2005 and 2007'. (2013) [https://www.pentagram.com/news/paula-scher-wins-national-design-award]; Chrysler Award for Innovation in Design (extra, found in the Pentagram bio) (2000) [https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher]; Beacon Award for integrated corporate design strategy, for the Public Theater identity (extra, Pentagram bio) (1996) [https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher]; SEGD Fellow (extra, Pentagram bio; also SEGD's own 2019 Fellow profile) (2019) [https://segd.org/?p=8105]; Royal Designer for Industry (RDI) (extra, Pentagram bio) (2021) [https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher]; D&AD Special President's Award (extra, Pentagram bio) (2022) [https://www.pentagram.com/about/paula-scher]
**Core question:** Does the Arabic typography itself carry one specific TabeebX idea, through scale, weight, placement, a single pink accent and (in video) motion, strongly enough to stop a thumb in the first second? Does the treatment come from the subject rather than a template, and does the piece still clearly belong to the TabeebX system?
**Principles (with evidence):**
- Type is the image. Letterforms carry spirit and emotion before anyone reads them, so typography should do the illustrating and not caption a picture. This comes from the advice of her teacher Stanislaw Zagorski, 'Illustrate with type'. Pentagram's page for her Type Is Image exhibition sums up her belief as 'words have meaning, and type has spirit'.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "I realised type had spirit and could be differentiated depending on how it was drawn and that when you attached it to words, it gave them various meanings based on what you were looking at. ... the typography became more important to me than the image because the type was the image." [Creative Boom: Paula Scher on falling in love with typography, timeless identities and what it takes to become a great designer](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/paula-scher/)
- The form must come from the subject. The typeface and its treatment should follow from what the thing actually is, so the design resonates and stands for something. A layout that would fit any subject has failed.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "It has to resonate, and it has to represent something. ... If you're going to design a logo for a railroad that was going to be turned into a park, it should have railroad type in it, so that's what we did." [Creative Boom: Paula Scher interview (identity and the High Line)](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/paula-scher/)
- Commit to one big, specific idea and protect it. In her account, vague, impressionistic, blurry or moody imagery took over because it was noncommittal, not because it worked. Simple designs that get amended by committee lose all their interest and joy.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "The big-image, big-idea album covers I designed and art-directed in the seventies were impossible to achieve in the eighties. The images that would be accepted with relative ease tended to be nonspecific, impressionistic, blurry, or moody. Ambiguity, a postmodern approach, worked stylistically because it is apolitical and noncommittal. ... They can focus on anything reasonably simple, and will amend it until all the interest and joy are removed or until they are out of time." [AIGA New York, 'Style Wars' by Paula Scher (first-person essay on a page introducing Make It Bigger; it reads as a book excerpt)](https://2001.aigany.org/ideas/features/scher.html)
- Make the thing the job is about big. Scale is a decision about meaning: whatever carries the message (the numbers in the Bloomberg project) gets blown up, and that scale holds up even when budget or space shrinks. The title Make It Bigger points the same way. Eye's review says it refers partly to her preference for working bigger and quotes 'I prefer to work bigger'. That is a second-hand quote and is not used as evidence here.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "One of my favorite projects was the Bloomberg LED. The budget got cut by 50 percent, but because we had made the numbers so big—because it was a job based on numbers—we just cut the jumbotron in half." [SEGD: 2019 SEGD Fellow Paula Scher—Exactly as Rendered (interview)](https://segd.org/?p=8105)
- Get it working in black and white first, then treat colour as a controlled variable. A strong type treatment holds up in black and white and so survives many colour combinations. If a design depends on colour to work, the type treatment underneath is weak.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "Most strong type treatments (if they work in black-and-white) work in a plethora of color combinations, so when an editor would indicate that he or she didn't like blue, green was possible." [AIGA New York, 'Style Wars' by Paula Scher](https://2001.aigany.org/ideas/features/scher.html)
- A system gets its weight from being whole. One visual voice runs through every touchpoint, down to the smallest ad and the ticket. Pieces that are each too individual lose 'the heft of everything being the same thing'.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "And what was different about it was the totality of it, was that I really became the voice, the visual voice, of a place in a way I had never done before, where every aspect -- the smallest ad, the ticket, whatever it was -- was designed by me. ... they never had the seriousness of the first identity because they were too individual, and they didn't have that heft of everything being the same thing." [TED: Paula Scher, Great design is serious, not solemn (official transcript)](https://www.ted.com/talks/paula_scher_great_design_is_serious_not_solemn/transcript)
- Serious, not solemn. Invention, change and a willingness to be imperfect matter more than polish. In her terms, solemn design is the correct, expected and over-perfected kind.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "Serious design is imperfect. It's filled with the kind of craft laws that come from something being the first of its kind. ... That's because the art of serious play is about invention, change, rebellion -- not perfection. Perfection happens during solemn play." [TED: Paula Scher, Great design is serious, not solemn (official transcript; 'craft laws' is as transcribed, probably 'craft flaws')](https://www.ted.com/talks/paula_scher_great_design_is_serious_not_solemn/transcript)
- Know the audience's world, reflect what it expects, then push those expectations forward 'just a little bit' without alienating anyone. Cut every piece of information that doesn't tell the audience something; for her, good design comes from reduction.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "What your job is, is to reflect those expectations because they matter to the people you’re talking to: that’s one role for your client, then your role is to elevate their expectations just a little bit, so that you’re looking at a certain set of expectations that a public has, and you want to pull them forward without alienating them by taking them too far. ... I have an ability to reject information that is useless. There are things that tell you something, and things that really don’t." [The Creative Store (NZ): Interview with Paula Scher (ahead of Semi Permanent Sydney)](https://thecreativestore.co.nz/creative-talk/design/interview-with-paula-scher/)
**Checks:** Thumbnail or first-second test (principles 1 and 4): shrink the frame to feed-thumbnail size, or freeze a reel at about 0.5 to 1 second. Exactly one Arabic hero word or phrase should read, and the type should be the image (or fused with it), not a caption over a photo. · Scale hierarchy (principle 4): the hero line (ideally 2 to 5 words, Cairo Bold) takes a dominant share of the frame, and the jump in size from hero to secondary text is obvious. Rule of thumb: at least about 3:1. That ratio is this lens's heuristic, not Scher's rule. The biggest element is the thing the piece is about: the service, the action, or a number taken from tabeebx-brand. · Form from subject (principle 2): the designer can say in one sentence why this treatment fits this topic. Examples: home visit, the type travels toward a door; lab results, the number is the hero; physiotherapy, the type stretches. Swap test: replace the topic with another service. **For TabeebX:** How to apply. (1) Arabic RTL and Cairo: Scher's 'type has spirit' fits Arabic's strong lettering tradition, but Cairo is a clean modern sans. Scher told It's Nice That that 'mostly the identity lives on in the Instagram posts of its audiences', which supports judging the piece as it appears in a feed, not as a standalone poster. The raised-arms logo can work as part of the typographic composition, but never recoloured or distorted. (4) Calibrate 'loud' for health: the Public was meant to be 'urban and loud' (TED), but Scher's milieu principle says to reflect the audience's expectations and push only a little. (5) A related principle that didn't fit in the list: in the SEGD interview Scher says 'my goal is to try to push something to defy the expectation of what it’s supposed to be... **Core question:** Does one declared, appropriate structure (a grid, two type sizes, functional Cairo weights, and colour that only signals) govern every element in every slide or frame? The result should be a piece that an Iraqi viewer understands at a glance without explanation and that is clearly one member of a TabeebX system, not a set of arbitrary decisions.
**Principles (with evidence):**
- One appropriate grid governs everything. The grid is the basic structure: it organises content, gives consistency and order, and there is exactly one right grid for each problem. A grid that is too fine is as good as none, one that is too coarse leaves too few choices, and a horizontal frame of reference is needed as well as columns.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "Nothing could be more useful to reach our intention than the Grid. The grid represents the basic structure of our graphic design, it helps to organize the content, it provides consistency, it gives an orderly look and it projects a level of intellectual elegance that we like to express. There are infinite kinds of grids, but just one - the most appropriate - for any problem. ... The basic understanding is that the smaller the module of the grid the least helpful it could be. ... Conversely a page with a coarse grid is a very restricting grid offering too few alternatives. ... Columns provide only one kind of consistency, but we also need to have an horizontal frame of reference to assure certain levels of continuity throughout the publication." [The Vignelli Canon (free PDF edition, hosted by RIT Vignelli Center), section 'Grids, Margins, Columns and Modules'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Syntactic consistency and discipline: a self-imposed set of rules keeps type, grid and images in the same relationships from page to page, with no fragmentation. Systems over happenstance.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "Syntactic consistency is of paramount importance in graphic design as it is in all human endeavors. Grids are one of the several tools helping designers to achieve syntactical consistency in graphic design. ... Discipline is a set of self imposed rules, parameters within which we operate. It is a bag of tools that allows us to design in a consistent manner from beginning to end. Discipline is also an attitude that provides us with the capacity of controlling our creative work so that it has continuity of intent throughout rather than fragmentation. Design without discipline is anarchy, an exercise of irresponsibility. ... I love systems and despise happenstance." [The Vignelli Canon, sections 'Syntactics', 'Discipline' and 'Conclusion'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Identity and diversity in a series, and sequence as cinema. Keep the identifiers constant and let content vary, which avoids both fragmentation and sameness. A multi-page piece is both a static spread and a cinematic sequence, and the layout should almost disappear.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "In a Corporate Identity Program enough diversity must be provided to avoid sameness and to increase attention. Too much diversity creates fragmentation - a very common disease of badly designed communication. Too much identity generates perceptive redundancy and lack of retention. ... A typical example would be a book series where the basic identifiers are constant and the specific illustration changes from cover to cover. ... A publication is simultaneously the static experience of a spread and the cinematic experience of a sequence of pages. ... We would say that if you see the layout, it is probably a bad layout!" [The Vignelli Canon, sections 'Identity and Diversity' and 'Sequence'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Typographic economy and white space as silence. Structure matters more than typeface choice. Use one or two type sizes at most, give each weight a function (never loudness), never deform or decorate type, and never let type act out the word. Strength comes from contrast of scale with white space, not from shouting.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "In other words, is not the type but what you do with it that counts. The accent was on structure rather than type. ... I don’t believe that when you write dog the type should bark! ... We do not like the use of type as a decorative element, and we are horrified by any type deformation. ... I love the play between a very large type size for headlines versus a much smaller type size for the body text, with proper white space in between. ... It is really the white that makes the black sing. ... Our first rule is to stick to one or two type sizes at the most. ... Type weights can be used to great advantage when dedicated to a specific function, rather than be used for color purposes or even worse as a phonetic analogy. Some people who talk loud and tend to scream trying to persuade you, love to increase the size and weight of type to make the message louder. That is exactly what I consider intellectual vulgarity ... In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable. White space provides the silence. ... Bad layouts have no space left for breathing - every little space covered by a cacophony of type-sizes, images, and screaming titles." [The Vignelli Canon, sections 'Typefaces, The Basic Ones', 'Type Size Relationships', 'Contrasting Type Sizes' and 'Layouts'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Colour is a signifier and identifier, not decoration. Keep the palette limited and articulated, and choose colours for appropriateness to the message.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "Most of the time we use color as a Signifier, or as an Identifier. Generally speaking we do not use color in a pictorial manner. ... It merely means that most of the time we like to use color to convey a specific message, therefore, we tend to use it more as symbol or as an identifier. ... as we do with typefaces, we have limited and articulated our palette to express the message in the clearest and most understandable way. ... Appropriateness is one of the rules we use in choosing colors knowing how effective it can be to use the right color at the right time." [The Vignelli Canon, section 'Color'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Semantics and pragmatics. Every element must carry meaning or have a precise purpose, with no arbitrary stripes or swashes. A piece that is not understood has failed, and it should stand by itself without explanation. Complexity is welcome, complication is not.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "It means to design something that has a meaning, that is not arbitrary, that has a reason for being, something in which every detail carries the meaning or has a precise purpose aimed at a precise target. How often we see design that has no meaning: stripes and swash of color splashed across pages for no reason whatsoever. ... Whatever we do, if not understood, fails to communicate and is wasted effort. ... Sometimes it may need some explanation but it is better when not necessary. Any artifact should stand by itself in all its clarity. ... We love complexities but hate complications! ... In the end, a design should stand by itself, without excuses, explanations, apologies." [The Vignelli Canon, sections 'Semantics', 'Pragmatics' and 'Responsibility'](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf)
- Information architecture, objective rather than trendy. Structure information into fixed zones with a small vocabulary of variables. His own health-sector example was pharmaceutical packaging: fixed horizontal bands (product name, description, company identity) with one colour assigned to each product. Do not import outside looks; find the solution inside the problem.. Evidence (fetched, quote): "‘We brought discipline to design,’ he claims. ‘We are systematic, logical and objective – not trendy. Trends kill the soul of design.’ ... The basis is that visual language has a structure but it has a vocabulary of variables ... We are information architects who structure information. ... The concept here was to use horizontal colour bands across the product line as a way of making more coherent the information about the product. This is critically important when designing pharmaceuticals for people. Our idea was to place on the top band the name of the product, on the middle band the product description; and save the bottom band for the company identity. Different colours are designated for different products. ... when you have a design to do, don’t look outside. Learn to look inside the problem, because you will find the solution is right there waiting for you to get it out." [Eye no. 83 (2012), 'Reputations: Massimo Vignelli', interview by R. Roger Remington](https://eyemagazine.com/feature/article/reputations-massimo-vignelli)

**Checks:** Declared grid: the Designer must state the grid for the format: outer margins, number of columns, and number of horizontal modules (for example, a 4:5 carousel slide or a 9:16 frame, with the grid set inside the platform UI-safe area). The critic overlays it and counts elements that sit off a column or module line. Target is zero. · One grid for the whole piece and series: margins, the headline zone, the logo position and size, and any slide number or progress mark stay in the same place on every carousel slide, story frame and video scene. · Appropriate grid: modules are coarse enough to give order and fine enough to hold the content. Look for a horizontal frame of reference (consistent rows or baselines), not just a centre line. · Type economy: count distinct type sizes per frame (target 2; a third only for a functional micro-line such as a source, legal line or handle) and across the piece. Cairo only. Bold carries the headline or signal and Medium carries supporting text. Every weight change has a function and none exists to make something louder. · Scale contrast gives power: each frame has one dominant element, clearly larger than the rest (the Canon's own example is heading type about twice the text size). Impact comes from contrast plus space, not from many big bold things. · White space as silence: a large share of each frame is deliberately empty navy or white. Text never touches the margins and never sits under Instagram or TikTok UI. One idea per frame. · Arabic RTL setting: right-aligned (ragged-left) text is the default axis, mirroring the Canon's flush-left default. Centring is used only if the whole system is centred by intent. Text blocks hang from a common top line. Line height is generous enough for Arabic dots and descenders. Arabic is never letter-spaced (tracking breaks the joined script). No decorative stretching, faux bold, skew, outline, shadow, 3D or warp. · Numerals and mixed script: one numeral system (Arabic-Indic or Western) is used consistently across the piece and series. Latin fragments such as a URL or handle sit on the same grid and baseline logic. · Colour as signifier: navy or white is the ground. Pink #EE396B appears once per frame and marks the single most important item (the key word, the number or the call to action). Blue #1B9CCE carries one assigned meaning and keeps it on every slide. No gradients, washes or extra hues without a coded meaning. · Semantics remove-test: for each element (line, shape, icon, image, sticker), ask what it means or does. If removing it loses nothing, it is a defect. · Pragmatics: each frame is understood in about 2 seconds without the caption. Video text cards hold long enough to read at Iraqi reading pace. **Anchors:** 10: Exemplary system piece. A declared grid inside the safe area, and every element on every slide or frame snaps to it. Two type sizes with a clear dominant element. It communicates, but it feels assembled rather than designed as a system. | 4: No discernible grid. **For TabeebX:** How to apply it: the critic scores how well the piece follows these documented principles. It writes 'Under the structure-and-restraint lens (from The Vignelli Canon)...' and never 'Vignelli would say' or anything in his voice. It quotes only the verified passages listed in the principles, with their URLs.

What not to take literally: his own typeface list (Helvetica, Bodoni, Garamond and others) and his primary-colour preference are personal choices, not the principle. The transferable rule is 'few faces, used structurally' and 'colour as a coded signal'. Cairo Bold and Medium is already the kind of economical single family the Canon argues for (designculture.it Q&A: 'What matters is the typographical structure, not the typeface!'), so judge how Cairo is used, not the font itself. His taste for narrow outside margins 'to increase the tension' must give way on vertical video, where the platform UI-safe area is the real outer margin.

Arabic RTL: mirror everything. The grid starts from the right edge, text is right-aligned (ragged left) by default, and 'hangs from the rule' becomes a common top line at the right. Arabic has no capitals, so hierarchy has to come from size, weight and space, which fits his two-size rule. Do not transfer his 'tight kerning' advice: never track or letter-space Arabic. Treat decorative stretching, outlines and warps as the 'type deformation' the Canon is horrified by. Pick one numeral system per series. Any 'next' or continuity cue on a carousel must agree with both the RTL reading direction and the platform's swipe, and should be fixed once as a system constant.

One pink accent: this is the clearest application of 'colour as Signifier/Identifier'. Pink means 'the one thing to notice' and appears once per frame. Navy is the ground, white is the space and text, and blue keeps one assigned meaning. His pharmaceutical packaging (fixed bands, one colour per product; Eye no. 83) is a direct model for a TabeebX service series: fixed zones for service name, description and identity, with colour coding that never changes meaning.

Vertical video and motion graphics: treat the frame sequence like his 'cinematic sequence of pages'. Keep the grid stable across cuts, let elements land and hold on grid positions, and use one transition vocabulary. 'When you write dog the type should bark' is the key red flag for kinetic typography.

Logo: the Canon's 'Equity' section argues for protecting established marks, so the raised-arms figure is never restyled.

Balance: his 1972 subway diagram, admired by designers but replaced in 1979 partly because riders disliked its indifference to geography, is the built-in caution. A score of 10 also needs instant comprehension by Baghdad families, not purity alone.

**For TabeebX:** How the lens applies to TabeebX:

(1) Arabic RTL: Chahine's core image is letters that 'hold hands' and lines that roll effortlessly. For TabeebX this means the critic first checks shaping integrity (joins, RTL order, intact dots). Kinetic typography must move whole words in reading order (right to left) and never pull connected letters apart. Her thesis literature review reports that the brain's right hemisphere struggles to tell apart Arabic letters that differ only by dots (Eviatar et al.). That is a sound reason to protect dots from crops, outlines and highlight boxes, but it is my application, not her rule.

(2) Cairo font: Cairo (Google Fonts) is generally described as a Kufi-based geometric sans. I know this from background knowledge and did not verify it in this run. Her MIT AgeLab glance study found open, humanist Frutiger needed less glance time than squared Eurostile. MyFonts' editorial text (not her words) says her Kufi-leaning designs suit display and shorter texts. So with Cairo, keep lines short and sizes generous, use Bold for short hooks and Medium for supporting text, and give space through line height and word spacing, not letter tracking. Her thesis tested only Naskh text faces in continuous reading, and its literature review notes that bold reads slower up close but is easier to tell apart at a distance. Her data therefore do not rank Cairo, and the critic must not claim they do.

(3) One pink accent: her complexity-cost finding supports a single accent. Pink goes on one whole key word per frame, never part of a connected word (a colour break can split Arabic shaping in some tools), and its contrast is checked on navy and white.

(4) Vertical video: 'glances are the new currency' maps straight onto Reels/TikTok and stories. Each text card must be readable within its hold time, with short, frequent Iraqi words, solid plates over footage and text clear of platform UI. Save expressive or slow-down typography for one deliberate emotional beat, per her own 'it really depends on the purpose of the design'.

(5) Bilingual: Arabic leads. The Latin wordmark 'TabeebX' and any numerals sit as matched companions, never dominant (her 'equal importance... respecting the integrity of each').

**Checks:** 1. Shaping integrity (hard gate). Check every Arabic word: letters join correctly in their initial, medial, final and isolated forms. Nothing is disconnected, reversed or rendered left to right. No dots or marks are missing, clipped or colliding, no boxes or missing-glyph squares appear, and no AI-generated pseudo-Arabic shows up inside imagery. All Arabic is live type set in the design tool, never letters baked into a generated image. · 2. Spelling and correctness. No spelling or grammar mistakes in the Arabic. The copy matches the file that passed lint-copy.mjs character for character (Boutros calls such errors in public type 'confusing'). · 3. Right-to-left composition. The whole piece reads right to left: the hook or headline sits at the reading start (top-right), and arrows, progress dots or bars, timelines, step numbers and 'before/after' or 'problem/solution' sequences all advance right to left. Read the sequence as an Iraqi reader would and confirm it doesn't reverse the meaning (the 'wealth to poverty' failure). · 4. No Latin habits on Arabic. No slanted or obliqued Arabic posing as italic, no letter-spacing (tracking) that breaks joins, no Latin-style single-baseline cramming. Emphasis comes from Cairo Bold against Medium, from size, or from the one pink accent. · 5. Leading and vertical rhythm. Arabic ascenders, descenders and dots vary in length (Boutros: letters 'hardly ever sit on the same baseline'). Line spacing should be generous enough that stacked lines never collide, and no text box clips the top dots or bottom tails. · 6. Bilingual harmony. Any Latin text (TabeebX, a URL, an English term) and all numerals use Cairo's own matched Latin or the supplied logo, never a third font. The Latin's weight and apparent height sit comfortably with the Arabic and don't overpower it. In consumer pieces the Arabic leads the hierarchy. · 7. Logo and brand mark. The TabeebX logo (human figure with raised arms) is the supplied file: not redrawn, stretched or recoloured, and with clear space around it. If the Arabic name appears beside it, it is balanced with the mark for style, weight and legibility, not typed in a default font. · 8. Concept native to the reader. The idea reads as made for Iraqi Arabic readers, not a translated Western template. Cultural symbols are checked: a Gulf News review of 'Arabic for Designers' cites the owl, a Western symbol of wisdom, which is 'associated with death in the Middle East'. · 9. Ornament earns its place. Calligraphic flourishes, arabesque patterns or 'Arabic-flavour' decorative fonts appear only if they serve the message (Boutros uses calligraphy only 'providing I feel it fits with my objectives'). Cairo stays the house voice. · 10. Heritage plus contemporary. The piece feels current and local, neither generic Gulf-luxury nor generic Western stock design. The designer's rationale names concrete type decisions (weights, sizes, leading, reading path), not adjectives like 'modern' or 'contemporary'. · 11. Motion: kinetic typography builds, wipes and slides run right to left. Text animates as whole words or phrases, never letter by letter (which exposes unjoined forms mid-animation). No baseline jitter, and each line holds long enough to read at phone size. Check frame by frame at the points where text is half-revealed. · 12. Legibility at real size. Judge on a phone at 1080x1920 (or 1080x1350 for feed posts), not zoomed in. The key line is readable at a glance, text is clear of platform UI overlays, and colour contrast is sufficient (see notes).
**Red flags:** Disconnected, isolated or reversed Arabic letters, or words in the wrong order. Classic broken right-to-left rendering from AI tools or a bad export. This caps the score at 2. · Pseudo-Arabic or garbled glyphs inside AI-generated images, or Arabic text baked into a generated image instead of set as live type. · Arabic headlines slanted to fake an italic, or letter-spaced Arabic. · Dots or descenders clipped by a text box, or lines colliding because the leading was copied from Latin. · Layout mirrored from a left-to-right template: arrows pointing left to right, progress bars filling left to right, a before-to-after sequence that reads backwards in Arabic. · More than one type family, a Latin wordmark or numerals that look heavier or taller than the Arabic, or mixed numeral systems in one piece. · Logo distorted, redrawn or recoloured, or the Arabic brand name typed in a random font next to the mark. · Decorative calligraphy, arabesque borders or 'Arabic-style' display fonts added for flavour with no message purpose. · A rationale that rests on 'modern', 'clean', 'contemporary' or 'inspired by' with no concrete type decisions behind it. · Kinetic text animated letter by letter, or wiped in left to right, on Arabic. · The pink accent applied to part of a joined word (it can break shaping in some tools), or used on several elements so it stops working as an accent. · Any spelling or grammar error in the Arabic.
**Anchors:** 10: The Arabic is flawless and reads as designed for Arabic, not translated. Leading is generous and nothing collides or clips. The Latin wordmark and numerals sit in visible harmony of weight and height. The logo is untouched and balanced. **For TabeebX:** HOW TO USE: The Design Critic applies these documented principles. It never speaks as Mourad Boutros, never claims his endorsement, and may quote him only with the exact sentences listed in this record, attributed to their sources. Principle 5 (the 'not a simple matter of translation' point) is the journalist's reported speech and must not be quoted as Boutros's words. Lead the score with check 1, because broken shaping or right-to-left order caps the score at 2.

ARABIC RTL: Read every slide and frame from top-right. Carousels: check sequence cues (numbered steps, arrows, 'swipe' prompts, dots) on a real phone in the Instagram app. Don't assume the app's swipe direction matches Arabic reading order, and avoid designs whose meaning depends on an element bleeding across a slide edge in one direction. WhatsApp images: same right-to-left rules; check that text survives compression at small size.

CAIRO: One family covers both Arabic and Latin. Set 'TabeebX', URLs and numerals in Cairo's own Latin (or use the logo file) rather than adding another Latin face. That is the practical version of Boutros's 'harmony, not compromise'. Build emphasis only from Cairo Bold and Medium plus size. Never use synthetic oblique or tracking on Arabic. Give Arabic lines more leading than you would Latin. Pick Arabic-Indic (٣) or Western (3) digits once per piece and keep to it (a TabeebX house heuristic, not a Boutros rule).

ONE PINK ACCENT (#EE396B): Apply it to one whole word or phrase, never part of a joined word, because a colour change mid-word can break letter joining in some tools; check the render. Contrast ratios I computed with the WCAG formula: pink on navy #181943 is about 4.32:1 (fine for large headline text, below 4.5:1 for small body text). White on pink is about 3.87:1 (large text only). White on navy is about 16.7:1. Blue #1B9CCE on navy is about 5.32:1. Body-size Arabic should be white on navy, not pink. Pink must work as emphasis, never as calligraphic ornament.

VERTICAL VIDEO (Reels/TikTok, kinetic type): Reveals, wipes and word-builds start on the right and move left. Animate whole words or phrases, never letter by letter. Scrub to every half-revealed frame and check for unjoined forms. Hold each line long enough to read at phone size. Keep Arabic inside the central safe area, clear of the caption/UI band at the bottom and the profile band at the top. Don't let right-aligned Arabic run to the far right edge, because the platform's button column can cover it (that column may switch sides with the viewer's app language). Burned-in captions face the same right-to-left and shaping checks.

## Craft rules

- **[Arabic type on screen]** Never apply letter-spacing (tracking) to Arabic. Set tracking to 0 in Canva, After Effects, Figma and CSS. To widen a line, change the size or the copy, never the tracking. (confidence 0.9). Evidence: Apple WWDC22 'Design for Arabic' (search-result excerpt): 'If the Arabic typeface you're using isn't fully optimized for letter spacing, make sure to use 0 tracking, or simply use our system font, which adds the correct letters linkage.' The CSS Text Level 3 draft (drafts.csswg.org/css-text/) says letter-spacing must not be applied between cursive-script letters when connections would break. Chromium shipped 'Ignore Letter Spacing in Cursive Scripts' (blink-dev intent-to-ship). Seen in search results only; pages not opened. [https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10034/](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10034/)
- **[Kinetic typography (Arabic)]** Never animate Arabic letter by letter (per-character animators, typewriter presets, split-text). Animate whole words or lines, or reveal intact text with a moving mask, so letters stay joined and keep their contextual shapes. (confidence 0.85). Evidence: Framer component documentation (search-result excerpt): letters 'are cursive - they change shape depending on their neighbours - and wrapping each one in its own element breaks the join.' The aescripts ArabicText page (aescripts.com/arabictext) notes 'certain directional animation presets like "Typewriter" don't work with RTL languages.' Adobe community threads report darker overlaps at joins when per-character opacity is animated, and an expert suggests counting words instead of letters. [https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/shape-safe-reveal/](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/shape-safe-reveal/)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Give Arabic more leading than Latin. Start with line-height of about 1.6–1.8 for body or caption text and about 1.25–1.4 for headlines, then check that dots and descenders don't collide between lines. Add more if you use tashkeel. (confidence 0.65). Evidence: Apple WWDC20 'The details of UI typography' (search summary): Apple applies more leading for Arabic because ascenders and descenders can encroach on surrounding lines. WWDC22 'Design for Arabic' (search summary): Arabic is taller with dots and diacritics, and heavy vocalisation needs more vertical space to avoid clipping. The numeric ranges come from practitioner blogs (e.g. voxire.com, learntype.app: 1.6–1.9 body, 1.3–1.4 headings) and are not Apple figures. Cairo's own vertical metrics were not checked. [https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10175/](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10175/)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Minimum Arabic text size on a 1080-px-wide canvas (feed, carousel, story, reel): body and caption at least about 46 px, supporting labels at least about 40 px, headlines usually 72 px or more. Check a phone-size preview before review. (confidence 0.5). Evidence: Derived. Practitioner guidance (search summary of this and similar guides) is that Arabic should go slightly larger than Latin, e.g. 'English: 16px. Arabic: 17 – 18px', because complex joined strokes and diacritics blur at small sizes (learntype.app). A 1080-px canvas shown full-width on a ~393-pt-wide phone is scaled ×0.364, so 17 CSS px ≈ 47 canvas px. The sources conflict (one says Arabic can be 1–2 px smaller depending on the font), and no Cairo-specific test was found. [https://dev.to/jubayer910/rtl-ui-design-for-arabic-apps-a-guide-for-uae-teams-3bfd](https://dev.to/jubayer910/rtl-ui-design-for-arabic-apps-a-guide-for-uae-teams-3bfd)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** Build hierarchy through weight and size contrast, not case or italics, which Arabic doesn't have. With only Cairo Bold and Medium allowed, make the headline about 1.6–2× the body size and limit a slide to three text levels: headline, body, label. (confidence 0.75). Evidence: Huda Smitshuijzen AbiFarès (search-result excerpt from the Khatt Foundation results; the exact page was not confirmed): 'Due to the absence of italics, caps, and small caps in the Arabic script, weight and proportional variations are crucial for creating complex visual hierarchy.' The 1.6–2× ratio and the three-level cap are derived house heuristics, not from the source. Two weights come from the tabeebx-brand skill (Cairo Bold / Medium). [https://khtt.net/en/id/598](https://khtt.net/en/id/598)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** When Arabic and Latin share a frame (e.g. the TabeebX wordmark, a URL), balance visual weight and colour instead of forcing equal heights. Use Cairo's own Latin so both scripts come from one family, and let the Arabic take the vertical room it needs. (confidence 0.65). Evidence: Khatt Font Project brief (search-result excerpt): 'The Arabic font and its Latin counterpart were to have the same visual size at the same point size', with shared stem weight and contrast. A typography.guru thread on bilingual signage recommends 'balancing the visual weight of the two scripts, rather than height', noting that Arabic has no true x-height. Montasser Drissi (It's Nice That): scripts shouldn't have to mimic each other. Cairo is one Arabic+Latin family (Kufi-based Arabic extending Titillium Web, per github.com/Gue3bara/Cairo). [https://www.khtt.net/en/page/1714/the-design-brief](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/1714/the-design-brief)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Set Arabic ragged, aligned right. Never justify Arabic in social graphics, and never insert tatweel (U+0640) to stretch words or fill a line. Use elongation only as a deliberate, drawn display gesture in a logo or headline, approved as part of the system. (confidence 0.6). Evidence: W3C Arabic Layout (alreq) telecon minutes (search summary): the group distinguishes tatweel (the U+0640 character) from true kashida, and notes that plain-text environments 'can probably only use tatweels and extra spaces'. Typographer John Hudson (www-style list, 2016) ranks kashida as a low-priority justification method. Pentagram's Doha extended strokes are custom-drawn display letterforms, not inserted tatweel. Social tools lack proper kashida justification, so ragged alignment is the safe choice (derived). [https://www.w3.org/2016/06/28-alreq-minutes.html](https://www.w3.org/2016/06/28-alreq-minutes.html)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Use tashkeel (short-vowel marks) only to disambiguate a word that could be misread, never as decoration or on whole sentences. (confidence 0.6). Evidence: Nadine Chahine, 'Reading Arabic: legibility studies for the Arabic script' (Leiden, 2012), eye-tracking with 72 readers (search summary): higher word-formation complexity reduced legibility, and short vowels added processing cost but gave extra clarity, which 'results in a reduced number of regressions' (search-result excerpt). Vowels help with ambiguity but cost reading effort, hence 'only where needed' (derived application). WWDC22 also warns that heavy vocalisation needs extra vertical space. [https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/handle/1887/20022](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/handle/1887/20022)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Numerals: use Eastern Arabic digits (٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩) in Arabic copy. Use Western digits only in phone numbers and handles, and never mix systems in one phrase. Never reverse digit order inside a number in RTL layouts (٢٤ stays ٢٤), and keep times like ٨:٣٠ intact. (confidence 0.8). Evidence: House rule: the iraqi-dialect skill says 'Eastern Arabic digits in Arabic copy … Western digits only in phone numbers and handles', and lint-copy.mjs enforces it. Apple HIG Right to Left (developer-rno.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/right-to-left; search summary) says Arabic may use either digit system depending on region, and numerals within a number keep their order. Caveat: Iraq's new licence plates switched to Western digits from June 2024 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Iraq), so real-world usage is mixed. The house rule is a brand choice and stays until Yaqoob changes it. [/home/user/marketing-agents/.claude/skills/iraqi-dialect/SKILL.md](/home/user/marketing-agents/.claude/skills/iraqi-dialect/SKILL.md)
- **[RTL reading order]** Latin words inside Arabic lines (TabeebX, URLs, @handles) stay left-to-right. Don't put a Latin word at the start or end of an Arabic line next to punctuation or a number. Check the rendered frame for punctuation that jumped sides, and in code add a right-to-left mark after the neutral character. (confidence 0.7). Evidence: W3C technique H34 (search summary): neutral characters (spaces, punctuation) between runs of different direction can be placed wrongly by the bidi algorithm. W3C's own Arabic example has an exclamation mark landing on the wrong side, fixed by inserting a Unicode right-to-left mark after it. Material Design bidirectionality (m1.material.io/usability/bidirectionality.html; search-result excerpt): 'any LTR words, such as a URL, will continue to be shown in an LTR format.' [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H34](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H34)
- **[RTL reading order]** Mirror anything that shows sequence or progress for Arabic, including motion: step 1 sits at the right, progress bars fill right to left, 'next' arrows and wipes or pushes travel right to left, and timelines read right to left. Do not mirror numbers, the logo, clocks, charts' digit order, or non-directional icons (phone, camera). Verify the carousel swipe cue on a phone with Arabic UI before using an arrow. (confidence 0.65). Evidence: Material Design bidirectionality (search summary): when direction flips, numbers and untranslated text are not mirrored, icons that show forward movement are mirrored, and icons that don't communicate direction (camera, clock, phone) stay unchanged. Applying this to kinetic-type wipes is derived. No source confirmed whether Instagram reverses carousel swipe direction for Arabic users (see gaps). [https://m2.material.io/go/design-bidirectionality](https://m2.material.io/go/design-bidirectionality)
- **[Arabic type on screen]** Treat Cairo as a display-friendly Kufi-style face. It is strongest in Bold for short headlines. For body copy use Medium at the minimum sizes above, never lighter weights (brand allows only Bold and Medium), and check small text for clogged counters on a phone. (confidence 0.55). Evidence: Cairo by Mohamed Gaber (search summary of the repository and listings): the Arabic is built on the Kufi calligraphic style and extends the Latin family Titillium Web. It is variable from 200 to 1000 weight. Lighter weights are suggested for body and heavier for display. Brand weights come from /home/user/marketing-agents/.claude/skills/tabeebx-brand/SKILL.md. The legibility advice is derived (Kufi is more geometric and compact than Naskh); no Cairo legibility test was found. [https://github.com/Gue3bara/Cairo](https://github.com/Gue3bara/Cairo)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** White on navy (#FFFFFF on #181943, 16.68:1) is the default for any text that must be read, at any size. It passes WCAG AAA. (confidence 0.95). Evidence: WCAG SC 1.4.3 (AA) requires 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large-scale text, and ratios must not be rounded (W3C Understanding page, search summary). Ratio computed here with the WCAG relative-luminance formula from the brand hex codes in the tabeebx-brand skill: navy/white = 16.68:1. [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** Pink on navy (or navy on pink) is 4.32:1. It fails AA for normal text, so use it only for large or bold display words (the single accent word or the CTA), never for body or captions. (confidence 0.95). Evidence: Computed with the WCAG formula: #EE396B vs #181943 = 4.32:1, below the 4.5:1 normal-text threshold and above the 3:1 large-text threshold. W3C: 4.499:1 fails, and thresholds are not rounded (search summary). [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** White on pink (3.87:1) and white on blue (3.14:1) are large-text-only pairings. For readable text on a blue field use navy text (5.32:1). Pink on white (3.87:1) is also large-text-only. (confidence 0.95). Evidence: Computed with the WCAG formula: white/#EE396B = 3.87, white/#1B9CCE = 3.14, navy/#1B9CCE = 5.32. Only navy-on-blue reaches the 4.5:1 AA normal-text threshold. Brand colours stay unchanged. Any darker tint would be Yaqoob's decision. [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** Never put pink and blue against each other: no pink text on blue, no blue text on pink, and no pink icon on a blue field (1.23:1). They are nearly the same lightness and will vibrate and disappear for low-vision viewers. (confidence 0.95). Evidence: Computed with the WCAG formula: #EE396B vs #1B9CCE = 1.23:1, far below every WCAG text threshold (3:1 minimum for large text). [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** What counts as 'large text' on a 1080-px social canvas: Cairo Bold at about 52 px or more (≈14 pt bold on a phone), or Medium at about 66 px or more (≈18 pt). Pairings that only pass at 3:1 (pink/navy, white/pink, white/blue) must meet these sizes. (confidence 0.55). Evidence: W3C Understanding 1.4.3 (search summary): 18-point text or 14-point bold qualifies as large-scale text. 18 pt = 24 CSS px and 14 pt = 18.67 CSS px. Converting to a 1080-px canvas shown full-width on a ~393-pt phone (×0.364) gives ≈66 px and ≈51 px. The conversion is derived, and W3C notes images need special handling ('roughly 19pt and 24pt' at 72 ppi). [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum)
- **[Hierarchy and contrast]** Exactly one pink element group per frame or slide: the one thing the eye should hit first. Everything else is navy, white or blue. (confidence 0.9). Evidence: House brand rule: tabeebx-brand lists pink #EE396B as the accent (CTAs, highlights, logo figure). The W42 carousel checklist (content/2026-W42/pieces/01-ig-carousel-message-to-door.md) requires 'exactly one pink group'. This matches the 'one accent' brief in the task. - **[Carousel and story layout]** Carousels: export every slide at 1080×1350 (4:5) at the same ratio, because the first slide sets the crop for all of them. Keep the cover's headline and key visual inside the centred 1012×1350 area (about 34 px clear on each side), because the profile grid now previews posts at 3:4. (confidence 0.7). Evidence: Third-party guides (search summary; Meta's own pages were off-limits): the profile grid moved from 1:1 to a taller 3:4 preview in early 2025, the first slide's ratio crops the rest, and one guide says the grid 'trims roughly 34 pixels from each side' (reeldrop.io / carousel guides). The 3:4 crop of 1080×1350 is 1012.5 px wide, consistent with 34 px per side (derived). The 3:4 figure is measured by third parties, not published by Meta. [https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/)
- **[Carousel and story layout]** Carousel structure: slide 1 is a hook (a specific claim, question or surprising fact, never a generic 'X tips' title), each following slide carries one idea, and the last slide has a single CTA. Aim for about 6–10 slides. Use carousels for topics that need explanation or context. (confidence 0.6). Evidence: Nieman Lab on the NYT (search summary): slide formats work best for complicated stories or ones that need context, and Instagram's carousel suits them. Vendor guides (reeldrop.io, carouselli.com; search summary): slide 1 does most of the work and generic 'X tips for Y' titles give no reason to swipe. One guide says most working carousels use six to ten slides (low-authority sources). [https://www.niemanlab.org/2021/07/the-new-york-times-is-using-instagram-slides-and-twitter-cards-to-make-stories-more-digestible/](https://www.niemanlab.org/2021/07/the-new-york-times-is-using-instagram-slides-and-twitter-cards-to-make-stories-more-digestible/)
- **[Carousel and story layout]** Stories (1080×1920): keep text and key visuals clear of the top ~250 px (account row) and the bottom ~340 px (reply bar, link sticker), with at least 65 px side margins. Put the link sticker or poll in the bottom band, below the headline. For story ads use Meta's 14% / 20–35% / 6% margins. (confidence 0.55). Evidence: Search summary across several third-party guides: 250 px top and 250–340 px bottom are the common community figures, 340 px bottom is the conservative choice, and Meta publishes no organic figure. Meta's ad guidance (14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides) is reported second-hand. The repo's motion-templates.md uses the stricter 220/500/180 band for Stories 'as a conservative choice'. - **[Safe zones]** Reels/Shorts/TikTok text and key visuals stay in the centred safe band: 220 px top, 500 px bottom, 180 px each side on 1080×1920, leaving a 720×1200 band from y=220 to y=1420. Keep side margins symmetric because the action rail and caption stack can sit on either side in RTL interfaces. For Reels ads, also respect Meta's 14% top / 35% bottom / 6% sides. (confidence 0.65). Evidence: Google Ads Help on Shorts ads (search summary): 'Keep key elements out of the areas where the channel name, caption, and engagement buttons reside.' The hook (question, surprising fact, action) lands in the first 1–2 seconds. (confidence 0.7). Evidence: Google's ABCD playbook / Think with Google (search summary): Attention comes first. Hook viewers in the first few seconds with action, a question or a surprising visual, using tight framing and quick pacing, often several shots in the first five seconds (ppc.land summary). - **[Kinetic typography timing]** Arabic reading time for kinetic text: hold each text card at least 0.45 s per Arabic word plus about 0.5 s to find it, with a 1.5 s minimum. Never ask the viewer to read more than ~2.5 words per second, and keep a story frame meant to be read without tapping to about 12 words or fewer. Very fast 5–7-frame word flashes are only for one- to three-word emphasis beats, never for information. (confidence 0.55). Evidence: Derived from three inputs, all seen in search summaries only. (1) Alhassan & Almustanyir, Clinical and Experimental Optometry 2024: Arabic mean maximum reading speed 171 ± 27 standard-length words per minute (≈2.85 words/s, declining with age). (2) BBC subtitle guideline copy (clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines): 'The recommended subtitle speed is 160-180 words-per-minute (WPM) or 0.33 to 0.375 second per word' for English, i.e. below maximum reading speed. Scaling Arabic similarly gives ≈130 wpm ≈ 0.45 s/word. (3) SBCC motion-type notes: no more than 3 words per second. Motionographer's 'Don't Blink' analysis: most words held 5–7 frames for punchy emphasis. Story photo default duration is disputed (5 vs 7 s), hence the conservative word cap. [https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-38402851](https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-38402851)
- **[Kinetic typography timing]** In/out motion: text enters in about 8–12 frames at 30 fps (≈250–400 ms) with an ease-out/decelerate curve, then sits completely still for its full reading window, then exits faster (≈5–6 frames, 150–200 ms) with an ease-in/accelerate curve. Prefer a fade or short slide that follows reading direction (right to left) over bounces, spins or scale-pops on Arabic. (confidence 0.55). Evidence: Atlassian Design motion (search-result excerpt): 'Make exit motion faster than entrances so dismissed elements don't block someone's workflow', with 'Transitions (150–400ms)'. Material 3 easing and duration (m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration; search summary): exits use shorter durations, e.g. a 500 ms enter paired with a 200 ms exit, with emphasized-decelerate for entering and emphasized-accelerate for exiting. These are UI-motion systems, so applying them to social video is a derived adaptation. 'Still while being read' is derived from the reading-time rule. [https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion)
