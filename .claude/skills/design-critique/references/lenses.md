# The 10 critique lenses

Each lens applies the **published principles** of one designer, as researched on 2026-10-18. The critic writes "under the Scher lens", never "Paula Scher says". It never quotes a designer except from `docs/research/design-panel-2026-10.md`, and that research is **not yet fact-checked** (see its header).

Score each lens 0–10 against its anchors. Half points are allowed.

## 1. Milton Glaser: Concept and Feeling: one true idea the viewer completes

**Core question:** What should the viewer feel, and what should they believe or do afterwards? Does one honest, appropriate idea, decoded in a moment, make them feel it, using just enough elements and no style for its own sake?

**Checks**
- Name the feeling and the shift. Before scoring, the critic writes one line: 'from [existing condition] to [preferred condition], feeling [X]'. Example: from 'worried about Mum's blood test and the traffic' to 'relieved, a nurse can come home', feeling reassurance. If the brief or the piece gives no clear answer, the piece cannot score above 6.
- One idea. State the idea in 7 Arabic words or fewer. Is there a single visual idea (a metaphor, juxtaposition, symbol or twist) rather than a headline sitting beside loosely related pictures or icons?
- Puzzle timing. Is there a small 'aha' the viewer completes themselves? On a static frame or a video's first frame it should land in about 1 to 2 seconds (TabeebX's adaptation, not Glaser's number). Fail it if the picture only repeats the headline literally (nothing to engage) or if it needs the caption to make sense (confusion).
- Just enough. Count the elements: headline, sub, image, icons, logo, CTA, decoration. Could any be removed without losing the feeling? Is anything missing that the idea needs, such as a human cue or a concrete Baghdad detail?
- Pink as the emotional pivot. Like the single red heart in I Love NY against cool type, the one #EE396B accent should sit on the semantic or emotional centre of the idea (the turning word, the symbol, or the CTA) in one place, against navy #181943, white and blue #1B9CCE. Fail it if pink is used as decoration or appears in several competing places.
- Appropriateness over style. Was the look chosen for this message and audience (Iraqi families, elders, worried caregivers) or borrowed from a trend (glossy AI renders, 3D blobs, glassmorphism, generic 'medical blue' templates)? Would the idea survive a change of style? If it wouldn't, the style is doing the work the concept should.
- Honest feeling. Does it acknowledge the real situation with dignity (worry, waiting rooms, distance, an ageing parent) rather than fake cheer or fear? Does every image match what TabeebX actually delivers? Check for: no implied outcomes, no AI or stock faces presented as real staff or patients, no fine-print disclaimers. Cross-check with health-ad-policy.
- Words as images. Do the 2 to 4 key Arabic words (Cairo Bold) carry imagery by themselves, with Cairo Medium in support? In kinetic typography, does the motion of the words express their meaning, with one key word per beat, rather than animating everything at once?
- Beyond professional. Is there one unexpected, ownable move that a competitor's template would not contain? Or is this a competent repeat of the last post?

**Red flags**
- Clip-art concept: stethoscope-heart, floating medical icons, smiling stock doctor or generic glossy AI imagery. Any clinic in any country could post it.
- Two or more ideas competing in one frame or one Reel. The idea can't be said in one short sentence.
- The visual just illustrates the headline word for word (zero puzzle). Or the opposite: viewers need the caption to understand it.
- Pink accent in three or more places, or used on decoration instead of the idea.
- Faked emotion: grinning models, staged joy. Or manipulative fear: sirens, blood, panic imagery.
- Untruthful signals: implied cures or results, invented testimonials, AI people passed off as real staff or patients, tiny 'results may vary' type.
- Trend-led style (gradients, particles, 3D blobs) doing the work a concept should.
- A Latin layout mechanically mirrored for RTL, so the eye meets the punchline before the setup.
- Motion with no meaning: everything animates, nothing is revealed, no setup and payoff.

|---|---|
| 10 | A single, ownable idea that makes the named feeling land in the first second or two. The viewer completes the 'aha' and feels it was their own thought. Every element is essential; pink sits exactly on the emotional pivot; RTL reading order delivers setup then payoff. It is truthful and specific to Baghdad families. People would remember it and want to share or save it, and it could not be mistaken for a competitor's post or a template. |
| 8 | A clear, appropriate idea with the right feeling that decodes quickly and is honest. One or two surplus elements, or execution that is slightly generic (the idea is stronger than the craft, or the reverse). A confident 'yes' with a hint of 'wow'. |
| 6 | Competent, on-brand and understood, but literal: headline plus a relevant picture. The viewer is informed rather than moved, and the style is borrowed rather than chosen. 'Good' in the sense that it accomplishes a task. Professional, and forgettable. |

**For TabeebX:** How to apply it: the critic applies these documented principles and must never speak as Glaser. Write 'Under the concept-and-feeling lens (from Glaser's published principles)...', never 'Glaser would say...', and quote only the evidence lines above.

Arabic RTL: the puzzle has to resolve in Arabic reading order. Setup at the top right, payoff towards the lower left. In a carousel, the twist sits on the swipe to the next slide (RTL). Don't import English puns or rebuses. The 'aha' must work in Iraqi dialect for the iraqi-dialect skill. Glaser's I Love NY works because each part must be translated, and an Arabic equivalent has to be decodable by Iraqi viewers, not by English speakers.

Cairo font: treat the 2 to 4 key words as the image ('the right three words', BYU 2006). Cairo Bold for them, Cairo Medium for support. Letterform play (for example, a letter's dot becoming a location pin or a heart) is allowed only if the word stays fully legible and the Arabic letter shapes are respected. No fake kashida stretching or distorted joins.

One pink accent: the direct analogy is the single red heart against neutral American Typewriter letters in I Love NY. The emotional symbol carries the one warm colour. The raised-arms logo figure (relief, welcome) can serve as that symbol, but only when the idea calls for it.

Vertical video: the decoding window is the first 1 to 2 seconds before the swipe, so the setup must be visible immediately and the payoff should come on a clear beat. Motion should reveal the idea (setup then twist), one key word per beat, inside platform safe zones. A Reel that is all motion and no reveal scores 4 or below.

Health truth: 'Tell the truth' and the 'results may not be typical' example map directly onto health-ad-policy. Anything implying results, or using AI or stock people as staff or patients, caps the score at 2 to 4 under this lens, however beautiful it is.

The score anchors (great, good, misses the target) echo Glaser's own scale as quoted by AP in 2000 (PBS obituary): great when it 'moves it in a certain way', good 'when it accomplishes a task', bad 'when it misses a target' (https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/milton-glaser-designer-of-i-love-ny-logo-dies-at-91, fetched).

Lower-confidence sources, used only as colour and not as evidence:
(a) 'There are three responses to a piece of design—yes, no, and WOW! Wow is the one to aim for.' This is attributed to Glaser in Poster House's description of the book 'Yes, No, WOW!' (https://shop.posterhouse.org/yes-no-wow-the-push-pin-studios-revolution.html, fetched). I found no primary source that dates it.
(b) On I Love NY, Glaser is quoted saying 'We know that the issue in all communication is moving the brain, and problems move the brain. This one makes everyone feel good because they have solved it.' I saw this only in a Ukrainian exam-prep republication (https://zno.osvita.ua/english/all/1045/, fetched), apparently of a Telegraph article I could not locate.
(c) The widely shared line 'the computer is to design what the microwave is to cooking' is unsourced, and AZQuotes flags it as disputed. Do not use it.

Research method: WebFetch failed on DNS for every site, so pages were read with curl and converted to text. AIGA and Cooper Hewitt returned 403 and Big Think was blocked, so the AIGA Medal citation rests on a search snippet.

## 2. Paula Scher: Poster Lens: type as image, scale and one accent

**Core question:** Does the Arabic typography itself carry one specific TabeebX idea, through scale, weight, placement, a single pink accent and (in video) motion, strongly enough to stop a thumb in the first second? Does the treatment come from the subject rather than a template, and does the piece still clearly belong to the TabeebX system?

**Checks**
- Thumbnail or first-second test (principles 1 and 4): shrink the frame to feed-thumbnail size, or freeze a reel at about 0.5 to 1 second. Exactly one Arabic hero word or phrase should read, and the type should be the image (or fused with it), not a caption over a photo.
- Scale hierarchy (principle 4): the hero line (ideally 2 to 5 words, Cairo Bold) takes a dominant share of the frame, and the jump in size from hero to secondary text is obvious. Rule of thumb: at least about 3:1. That ratio is this lens's heuristic, not Scher's rule. The biggest element is the thing the piece is about: the service, the action, or a number taken from tabeebx-brand.
- Form from subject (principle 2): the designer can say in one sentence why this treatment fits this topic. Examples: home visit, the type travels toward a door; lab results, the number is the hero; physiotherapy, the type stretches. Swap test: replace the topic with another service. If the layout still works unchanged, it is generic. Mark it down.
- Black-and-white test (principle 5): convert to greyscale and the composition, hierarchy and legibility should still hold. Navy or white grounds with white or navy type should carry the piece without colour.
- One accent (principle 5): pink #EE396B appears on one word or one element, the one that carries the point, and nowhere else. It is not a background wash, not on several items, and not combined with extra accent colours. Blue #1B9CCE is a secondary ground or support, never a competing highlight.
- Commitment (principle 3): the message is concrete (what, for whom, what to do next). No vague slogan over a moody, blurry, stock-looking or AI-looking image. No signs of committee dilution, such as every element hedged, shrunk or 'safe'.
- Reduction (principle 8): count the text and graphic elements. Remove anything that tells the viewer nothing: stacked badges, icon rows, repeated logos, multiple phone numbers, hashtags in the artwork, decorative shapes.
- System weight (principle 6): put it in a 3x3 grid with the last eight TabeebX pieces. It should read as the same voice: Cairo Bold/Medium only, palette discipline, consistent logo placement, a recurring typographic move. Variation should sit inside a series look, not be a one-off.
- Expectation shift (principles 7 and 8): compared with the usual Iraqi clinic or health post (smiling stock doctor, gradients, icon clutter), the piece should pull the category forward a little and still be instantly legible and respectful to an older Baghdadi family member.
- Where the eye goes (TED: 'New Yorkers look at their feet'): design for where people actually look on a phone. Hero type sits inside 9:16 safe areas, clear of platform UI at the top, the bottom caption and CTA band and the right-hand action rail. In RTL the composition reads from the top right.
- Motion as performance (principle 1, the 'Bring in Da Noise' precedent): in kinetic typography the movement expresses what the word means. The hero word holds still long enough to read. Animation never breaks Arabic letter joins or reverses reading order.
- Arabic craft: letters are correctly joined, there is no Latin-style tracking on Arabic, no fake bold or italic, no third typeface, and any kashida elongation is deliberate and expressive rather than filler. Numerals follow one consistent convention throughout.

**Red flags**
- Type as an afterthought: a small centred caption sitting on a stock photo or AI image that is doing all the work.
- Two or more headlines at similar sizes competing, with no clear hero.
- Template look: the piece could belong to any clinic or app, and swapping the topic changes nothing.
- Pink used on several elements, as a gradient or wash, or alongside other accent colours. Colour is propping up a weak composition that falls apart in greyscale.
- Noncommittal mood imagery with a generic line (for example 'your health matters').
- Over-polished 'solemn' work: technically clean, every choice safe, nothing anyone would remember.
- Clutter that tells nothing: icon rows, badges, several CTAs, hashtags in the art.
- Off-system pieces: fonts other than Cairo, off-palette colours, a stretched or recoloured logo, a one-off style that breaks the grid.
- Broken Arabic: disconnected or reversed letters, LTR layout logic, per-letter animation that breaks joins, text clipped by platform UI.
- 'Loud' tipping into alarm or fear about symptoms. Loud here means scale and clarity, not panic. health-ad-policy takes precedence.
- The critic writing as if it were Scher ('I would...') or inventing Scher quotes. Critique must cite the principle, not impersonate her.

**For TabeebX:** How to apply. (1) Arabic RTL and Cairo: Scher's 'type has spirit' fits Arabic's strong lettering tradition, but Cairo is a clean modern sans. Expression should therefore come from scale, Bold-against-Medium contrast, cropping, stacking, tight line breaks, colour blocks and motion, not from distorting letterforms or breaking joins. Kashida elongation is allowed only as a deliberate gesture (compare Pentagram's stretched Knockout for the Public), never as filler. Do not apply Latin letter-spacing to Arabic. The RTL entry point is the top right, so put the hero there or in the upper-centre band. (2) One pink accent: the closest Scher precedent is Pentagram's Taming of the Shrew/Tartuffe posters, which 'singled out the words "lust," "shrew" and "tart" in a degraded fluorescent red' (https://www.pentagram.com/work/the-public-theater). Put pink on the single word that carries the point. Navy with white gives the black-and-white backbone; blue is a secondary ground. (3) Vertical video and motion: the 1995 Bring in 'Da Noise posters had 'letters radiate from a photograph of a tap dancer' (It's Nice That, https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/paula-scher-pentagram-twenty-five-years-at-the-public-graphic-design-publication-270120). Typeroom notes that the 2018 Shakespeare posters were 'originally conceived to appear as animated graphics' (https://www.typeroom.eu/article/shakespeare-park). So kinetic typography is in scope: the type should perform the word's meaning, then hold still long enough to read. Keep hero type inside the 9:16 safe areas. Scher told It's Nice That that 'mostly the identity lives on in the Instagram posts of its audiences', which supports judging the piece as it appears in a feed, not as a standalone poster. The raised-arms logo can work as part of the typographic composition, but never recoloured or distorted. (4) Calibrate 'loud' for health: the Public was meant to be 'urban and loud' (TED), but Scher's milieu principle says to reflect the audience's expectations and push only a little. For Baghdad families and home healthcare that means bold scale and clarity with warmth and trust, never alarm. health-ad-policy and tabeebx-brand facts override this lens, and big numbers must come from data files, never invented. (5) A related principle that didn't fit in the list: in the SEGD interview Scher says 'my goal is to try to push something to defy the expectation of what it’s supposed to be... Not in a sense it’s impractical, or if it doesn’t function', and that she dislikes jobs with 'tight, rigidly defined guidelines. Why bother to design it? Just pop it into the computer and print it out.' (https://segd.org/?p=8105). This is a useful warning about template-filled AI output. (6) Source caveats: two widely repeated Scher lines, 'The Public Theatre identity is based on being extremely loud and visible and urban' and 'I've never been a refiner; my best work has been big bold strokes that came very quickly', were seen only second-hand on Typeroom (the first is attributed to a Hillman Curtis interview; the second's original source isn't given). Eye's review only quotes 'I prefer to work bigger' from Make It Bigger. Treat all three as lower confidence and don't cite them as primary. Eye's reviewer (Matt Soar) also criticised the book's adages as thin, so the critic should apply the principles through concrete checks, not slogans. (7) Fact correction: Pentagram, SEGD, Creative Boom and designboom give 1991 as the year Scher joined Pentagram, not 1990. (8) Method note: WebFetch could not resolve hosts (DNS errors) in this session, so pages were downloaded with curl and read as text. No facebook, instagram or tiktok domains were accessed.

## 3. Massimo Vignelli: Structure and Restraint

**Core question:** Does one declared, appropriate structure (a grid, two type sizes, functional Cairo weights, and colour that only signals) govern every element in every slide or frame? The result should be a piece that an Iraqi viewer understands at a glance without explanation and that is clearly one member of a TabeebX system, not a set of arbitrary decisions.

**Checks**
- Declared grid: the Designer must state the grid for the format: outer margins, number of columns, and number of horizontal modules (for example, a 4:5 carousel slide or a 9:16 frame, with the grid set inside the platform UI-safe area). The critic overlays it and counts elements that sit off a column or module line. Target is zero.
- One grid for the whole piece and series: margins, the headline zone, the logo position and size, and any slide number or progress mark stay in the same place on every carousel slide, story frame and video scene.
- Appropriate grid: modules are coarse enough to give order and fine enough to hold the content. Look for a horizontal frame of reference (consistent rows or baselines), not just a centre line.
- Type economy: count distinct type sizes per frame (target 2; a third only for a functional micro-line such as a source, legal line or handle) and across the piece. Cairo only. Bold carries the headline or signal and Medium carries supporting text. Every weight change has a function and none exists to make something louder.
- Scale contrast gives power: each frame has one dominant element, clearly larger than the rest (the Canon's own example is heading type about twice the text size). Impact comes from contrast plus space, not from many big bold things.
- White space as silence: a large share of each frame is deliberately empty navy or white. Text never touches the margins and never sits under Instagram or TikTok UI. One idea per frame.
- Arabic RTL setting: right-aligned (ragged-left) text is the default axis, mirroring the Canon's flush-left default. Centring is used only if the whole system is centred by intent. Text blocks hang from a common top line. Line height is generous enough for Arabic dots and descenders. Arabic is never letter-spaced (tracking breaks the joined script). No decorative stretching, faux bold, skew, outline, shadow, 3D or warp.
- Numerals and mixed script: one numeral system (Arabic-Indic or Western) is used consistently across the piece and series. Latin fragments such as a URL or handle sit on the same grid and baseline logic.
- Colour as signifier: navy or white is the ground. Pink #EE396B appears once per frame and marks the single most important item (the key word, the number or the call to action). Blue #1B9CCE carries one assigned meaning and keeps it on every slide. No gradients, washes or extra hues without a coded meaning.
- Semantics remove-test: for each element (line, shape, icon, image, sticker), ask what it means or does. If removing it loses nothing, it is a defect.
- Pragmatics: each frame is understood in about 2 seconds without the caption. Video text cards hold long enough to read at Iraqi reading pace. Nothing clever or abstract asks the viewer to decode a private system (the lesson of his 1972 subway diagram).
- Motion system: elements enter, settle on grid positions and hold. One set of transitions, easing and durations is used for the whole piece. Words and lines move as units. Type never acts out its meaning (no shaking for 'pain', no bouncing letters). The underlying grid stays put across cuts.
- Sequence: the carousel or video reads as a cinematic sequence with varied rhythm (for example a dominant headline frame, then an information frame, then a call-to-action frame) while the identifiers stay constant. The last frame resolves without introducing a new style.
- Logo equity: the raised-arms figure is unaltered (no redraw, stretch, effects or off-palette recolour) and appears at a fixed system position and scale.
- Timelessness: no imported trend look such as glitch, neon gradients, meme fonts, heavy 3D emoji or a copied competitor template. The piece should still look correct in two years.

**Red flags**
- No identifiable grid: elements placed by eye, and margins or the headline position change from slide to slide.
- Everything centred by default, mixed with randomly offset elements, or Arabic text set flush-left inside an RTL layout.
- More than three type sizes in a frame, fonts other than Cairo, or weights used for loudness.
- Letter-spaced Arabic, decorative stretching of words, outline, shadow, 3D, skew or warped text, or faux bold.
- Pink used in several places per frame, as a background wash or as decoration. Off-palette colours or meaningless gradients.
- Swooshes, blobs, random lines, stock medical icons or stickers that carry no meaning ('stripes and swash of color' in the Canon's terms).
- Crowding: a 'cacophony of type-sizes, images, and screaming titles', text at the edges or under the platform UI, several ideas in one frame.
- Kinetic type that acts out the word, or different transition styles in every scene.
- Logo redrawn, recoloured, distorted or placed differently from piece to piece.
- Mixed numeral systems or inconsistent formats for phone numbers and prices.
- A piece that only makes sense with the caption or a voice-over explanation.
- Over-correction: a minimalism so rigid or abstract that the Iraqi viewer cannot read the message or the human warmth is lost (purity over comprehension).

**For TabeebX:** How to apply it: the critic scores how well the piece follows these documented principles. It writes 'Under the structure-and-restraint lens (from The Vignelli Canon)...' and never 'Vignelli would say' or anything in his voice. It quotes only the verified passages listed in the principles, with their URLs.

What not to take literally: his own typeface list (Helvetica, Bodoni, Garamond and others) and his primary-colour preference are personal choices, not the principle. The transferable rule is 'few faces, used structurally' and 'colour as a coded signal'. Cairo Bold and Medium is already the kind of economical single family the Canon argues for (designculture.it Q&A: 'What matters is the typographical structure, not the typeface!'), so judge how Cairo is used, not the font itself. His taste for narrow outside margins 'to increase the tension' must give way on vertical video, where the platform UI-safe area is the real outer margin.

Arabic RTL: mirror everything. The grid starts from the right edge, text is right-aligned (ragged left) by default, and 'hangs from the rule' becomes a common top line at the right. Arabic has no capitals, so hierarchy has to come from size, weight and space, which fits his two-size rule. Do not transfer his 'tight kerning' advice: never track or letter-space Arabic. Treat decorative stretching, outlines and warps as the 'type deformation' the Canon is horrified by. Pick one numeral system per series. Any 'next' or continuity cue on a carousel must agree with both the RTL reading direction and the platform's swipe, and should be fixed once as a system constant.

One pink accent: this is the clearest application of 'colour as Signifier/Identifier'. Pink means 'the one thing to notice' and appears once per frame. Navy is the ground, white is the space and text, and blue keeps one assigned meaning. His pharmaceutical packaging (fixed bands, one colour per product; Eye no. 83) is a direct model for a TabeebX service series: fixed zones for service name, description and identity, with colour coding that never changes meaning.

Vertical video and motion graphics: treat the frame sequence like his 'cinematic sequence of pages'. Keep the grid stable across cuts, let elements land and hold on grid positions, and use one transition vocabulary. 'When you write dog the type should bark' is the key red flag for kinetic typography.

Logo: the Canon's 'Equity' section argues for protecting established marks, so the raised-arms figure is never restyled.

Balance: his 1972 subway diagram, admired by designers but replaced in 1979 partly because riders disliked its indifference to geography, is the built-in caution. A score of 10 also needs instant comprehension by Baghdad families, not purity alone.

## 4. Paul Rand: Identity and Form: one idea, simply made, signed as TabeebX

**Core question:** Does this piece carry one clear idea in the simplest form that could hold it? Does it sign that idea unmistakably as TabeebX, with the raised-arms mark in its true basic form? Would it still read and still look right at thumbnail size, in one colour, and two years from now?

**Checks**
- Idea first: state the piece's single idea in one sentence. If you can't, or the visual only decorates the headline, the form is not developing an idea. Score down hard.
- Mark integrity: the raised-arms figure keeps its basic form. No redrawing, stretching, rotating or re-posing the arms, outlines, gradients, shadows, glows, or fills outside navy #181943, white, blue #1B9CCE or pink #EE396B. Treatment and context may change (scale, crop, placement, a reveal in motion); the form may not.
- Identify, don't describe: the mark is not dressed up with stethoscopes, hearts, crosses or pills to 'explain' healthcare. Explanation belongs to the content. The logo only signs it.
- Mark used actively or just stamped: does the mark (or one of its parts, such as the raised-arms gesture) take part in the idea, as Rand used trademarks as illustrative devices? Or is it a tiny corner stamp? Active use done well scores higher; a missing or illegible mark scores lower.
- Reduction test: remove each element in turn (icon, line, badge, second photo, extra text). If nothing is lost, it is superfluous. Count the pink: exactly one focal element (the key word, the CTA, or one shape) carries #EE396B.
- Seven quick tests on the whole piece and the logo lock-up. Distinctive: would it be mistaken for a competitor's or a template post? Visible: is it readable at a quarter size in the feed? Usable: does it work as an IG carousel tile, a 9:16 Reel/TikTok frame and a compressed WhatsApp image? Memorable: can you describe it after 2 seconds? Universal: is it clear to a Baghdad family audience? Durable and timeless: is there no trend effect that will date it within a year?
- One-colour and small-size test: the lock-up still holds as white on navy, as a single colour, and at profile-avatar and story-sticker sizes.
- Consistent construction: corner radii, stroke weights, angles and spacing follow one geometric logic that is compatible with the logo's geometry. Elements align to a grid. Type uses only Cairo Bold and Cairo Medium, with a clear two-to-three-step size hierarchy.
- Contrast and lively balance: there is deliberate contrast of size, value (navy against white), and photo against flat shape. Balance is purposeful and asymmetric where that helps, not dead-centre by default.
- Play within limits: the brand constraints are treated as the rules of the game (navy field, one pink surprise, Cairo, the mark) and used to make an everyday health moment uncommon. Wit, if present, must not make light of illness or patients.

## 6. Erik Spiekermann: Readable Type System

**Core question:** On a phone, at scrolling speed, can an Iraqi viewer read this piece at a glance and know what to do? - Spacing system: line space is clearly larger than word space and leaves room for Arabic dots and marks. Spacing is the same across all slides and frames. If size or line length changed, the other spacing values were adjusted too.
- Hierarchy: no more than 3 type sizes and 2 Cairo weights per frame, with clearly different size steps. Bold or the accent marks emphasis on one element, not everything.

- Numerals: phone numbers, prices, dates, durations and doses are large, robust and in one numeral system per piece (all Arabic-Indic or all Western). Phone numbers render in the right digit order inside RTL lines (check on device).

- Voice: the look comes from TabeebX's own system (Cairo at house sizes and weights, navy/pink/blue, house spacing and layout), not a generic template. No extra display, script or trendy fonts beside Cairo.
- Optical alignment: any Latin text, the brand name or numerals inside Arabic lines look the same size and weight as the Arabic. Edges and centres look aligned to the eye, whatever the measurements say.
- Motion (vertical video): each text beat stays on screen long enough to read it fully at normal pace. One kinetic device is used for meaning, not on every word. Arabic is never letter-spaced, split or distorted mid-word. All text sits inside the platform safe zones, clear of UI overlays.

**Core question:** In the first second, does this piece state one idea that grows out of TabeebX's message? - Contrast and the landing beat: flowing moves are answered by locked-off still holds, and the reverse. There is at least one deliberate pause on the most important word or image, and that is where the single pink accent appears.
- Sound sync: text arrivals and cuts land on beats or hits in the music or voiceover. The key word lands on the strongest hit. Voiceover and on-screen text say the same thing at the same moment. With sound off, the piece still reads fully.
